How this worksWhere the data comes from and how calls are kept honest

How stocks are picked. Every weekday before the market opens, the tracker scans US stocks worth $20 million to $2 billion that trade at least 300,000 shares a day. It ranks them by how much unusual attention they're getting: trading volume compared with normal, the jump in Reddit mentions, and the size of yesterday's price move. The top 10 are locked in before any call is made, so every pick counts toward the score.

Then it gathers fresh information on each pick: its price and trading activity, company filings with the SEC (such as plans to sell new shares, or insiders buying), Reddit chatter, Google searches, and how much of the trading was people betting against it (short selling).

A short list of simple rules turns that into a call: will the stock close higher or lower than yesterday, by tomorrow and by the end of a week? Confidence shows how strongly the rules agree. Most days most stocks have weak signals, so "low" confidence is common, and that's an honest answer.

Calls can never be changed after they're made. Each one is locked with a timestamp, chained to the one before it, and saved publicly on GitHub every day, so the track record can't be quietly edited.

This is an experiment, not investment advice. Small stocks are risky and a few weeks of results can be luck.
